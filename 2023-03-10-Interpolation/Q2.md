## Q2

### (A)

$$
E(v)=\dfrac{mc^2}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}
$$

Take the log of both sides, we have
$$
\log E(v)=\log mc^2-\dfrac{1}{2}\log(1-v^2/c^2)
$$
Take the derivative of both sides and repeat these 2 steps, we have
$$
\dfrac{E'(v)}{E(v)}=\dfrac{v}{c^2-v^2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \log E'(v)-\log E(v)=\log v-\log(c^2-v^2)
$$

$$
\dfrac{E''(v)}{E'(v)}-\dfrac{E'(v)}{E(v)}=\dfrac{1}{v}+\dfrac{2v}{c^2-v^2}=\dfrac{c^2+v^2}{v(c^2-v^2)}
$$

Let $v=0$, we have $E(0)=mc^2$, $E'(0)=0$, $E''(0)=m$. Simplify the 2 equations, we have
$$
(c^2-v^2)E''(v)=E(v)+3vE'(v)
$$
By the derivative formula, take the first & second order derivatives of both sides, we have
$$
-2vE''(v)+(c^2-v^2)E'''(v)=4E'(v)+3vE''(v)\\
-2E''(v)+2(-2v)E'''(v)+(c^2-v^2)E''''(v)=E''(v)+(6E'''(v)+3vE''''(v))
$$
Let $v=0$, we get $E'''(0)=0,E''''(0)=9m/c^2$, therefore 
$$
E(v)\approx E(0)+\dfrac{1}{1!}E'(0)v+\dfrac{1}{2!}E''(0)v^2+\dfrac{1}{3!}E'''(0)v^3+\dfrac{1}{4!}E''''(0)v^4\\=mc^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2+\dfrac{3}{8c^2}mv^4
$$

### (B)

$$
KE_c=E(v)-mc^2\approx(mc^2+\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2+\dfrac{3}{8c^2}mv^4)-mc^2=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2+\dfrac{3}{8c^2}mv^4
$$

### (C) 

A reasonable mass for a commercial jet is <u>127000 pounds ≈ 57606.2 kg</u>

A reasonable cruising speed is <u>926 km/h ≈ 257.2 m/s</u>

Source: [Cruise (aeronautics) - Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_(aeronautics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_(aeronautics))

#### i.

$$
KE_c\approx\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2+\dfrac{3}{8c^2}mv^4\approx\dfrac{57606.2\times 257.2^2}{2}+\dfrac{3\times 57606.2\times 257.2^4}{8\times (3\times 10^8)^2}(J)\approx1905378062.7(J)
$$

#### ii.

$$
KE=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2\approx\dfrac{57606.2\times 257.2^2}{2}\approx 1905378062.7(J)
$$

#### iii.

Note that the result from i. and ii. are exactly the same even we write them to the first decimal place. That's to say, the relative difference of the 2 values is less than $10^{-10}$, which is extremely small. The speed of the plane is too small, therefore we need not to consider the relativity effects.
