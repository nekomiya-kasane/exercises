After completing the course, I am deeply impressed by the essay *“It’s a Skill That Can Be Trained”: How Chinese Parents View Mathematics and What This Means* by Angel Mok. Possibly mainly because I’m a Chinese familiar with the social consciousness the article depicted, and it prompts me to reflect on the education, especially mathematics, science and technology education I received throughout the past few years, from a more objective perspective.

The essay notices that fact that the Asian, especially Chinese students tends to have better mathematics ability and demonstrate more outstanding performance in international mathematics assessments. Instead of attributing this to some innate factor, the author advocates cultural background to be the key determinant. The evidence is that Chinese parents have higher expectations and are more involved in children’s mathematical learning in schools, and they usually see mathematics as a skill that can be cultivated rather than a talent. This interpretation of the findings would help breaking the cycle of math anxiety.

Actually I don’t fully support the opinion that no student has an inborn advantage over another in mathematics learning. It is self-evident that the the appearance and ability are determined and influenced both by genes (inborn abilities) and acquisition efforts made afterwards - to some extent, the latter one is more essential. But I’m still surprised to realize that many people treat mathematics as a talent.

When I was young, I was taught that some classmates may be very smart compared to me by my teachers and parents. They are just illustrating a fact and convincing me that I should not be depressed or sullen due to this. Correspondingly, they said one should only be compared with himself/herself, trying best is already worth praising. Not only MY parents thought so, but the common consciousness of the society, from the leaders to the ordinary workers, from the past to now, thinks so. And not only mathematics, but also all of the other knowledges, can be learnt, improved and mastered while growing up. They seldomly believe in destiny and fate, ever the illiterates would encourage the students to “read books, study and listen to the teacher” and to make use of what they learned.

To be honesty, they are right. When I start my college life, and dived into higher level mathematics, such as measure theory, set theory, and real analysis, I benefit a lot from the thoughts and intuitions  trained throughout my teenager time, by completing thousands of exercises, read hundreds of books. The experiences of school-wide and national competitions prompt me to get to know more categories of the application of mathematics. For example, the application of complex numbers to evaluate the consequent multiplication of cosines and sines $\displaystyle\prod_i\cos\dfrac{2\pi i}{n}$. These small tricks and background knowledges are quiet inspiring. Also we should mention that practically, the achievements of Asian students have proved the success of such mathematics education.

Therefore, besides one of the common values that, very student deserves equal opportunities to develop their mathematical skills. Innovative teaching methods, engaging curriculum, and supportive learning environments are essential in achieving this. Teachers should strive to create a learning environment that fosters a love for the subject and encourages students to explore and experiment with mathematical concepts.

Moreover, in terms of math anxiety, which can be a major obstacle for many students, teachers can help students overcome their fears and develop a positive attitude towards mathematics. They’re not untalented, they just haven’t get in touch with enough materials and experiences, and not hardworking or trained enough. Even if at last, they cannot become a talent, they can master enough mathematic skills and application methods, which they can apply to their lives. And teachers realizing the point can help student to achieve this. This is my recommendation.

In conclusion, mathematics abilities can be trained, the education system should adapt to this assumption and help students to master mathematics in a more practical way.
